# Episode I — curated visual reference set 05: axes, helmets and relics

Internal art library only. Individual assets must be recreated or cleanly exported later, in the established game style.

## Approved directions

- **Axes:** this is the strongest weapon sheet so far. The single- and double-bladed silhouettes read well; use plain iron-and-wood axes for Common, restrained heraldry for Rare, and elemental or ornate forms only for high rarity.
- **Helmets:** leather caps, simple kettle helmets and practical visors are good early-game equipment. Distinctive winged, horned, plated and masked helmets provide later rare and named-item directions.
- **Relics:** timepieces, seals, books, lanterns, rune stones, saintly miniatures, compass motifs and carefully limited magical containers support the King Overtime story particularly well.

## Blunt weapon handling

The two blunt-weapon sheets remain **quality-warning references**, not a production source. Some ordinary mace and hammer silhouettes are clean enough to inspire a later reconstruction, but every flail and chained head requires a separate anatomy check. Do not preserve a design just because its rarity effects look impressive.

## Visual constraints

- Avoid horns, spikes, skulls, frost and glowing rune effects as generic decoration.
- High-rarity details must tell a specific story or mechanic; they are not a substitute for a coherent weapon shape.
- For helmets, preserve face-opening, neck protection and silhouette consistency before adding ornament.
